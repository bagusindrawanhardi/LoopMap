# Variable: US Military Credibility Index

- unit: Index 0–100 (composite of ally confidence surveys, adversary behavior, red-line enforcement record)
- related_loops: L-04, L-03

## Definition

A composite measure of how credibly US adversaries and allies believe the United States will use military force in defense of its stated interests and commitments. High credibility means adversaries refrain from crossing US red lines; allies maintain security dependence on US guarantees. Low credibility means adversaries probe US commitments; allies hedge toward alternative security arrangements.

## Unit

Composite index (0–100): components include ally confidence surveys (State Dept), adversary behavior proxies (attack frequency, red-line testing), force posture assessments, and expert consensus ratings (RAND, IISS).

## Delays

12–24 months for credibility to substantially rebuild after erosion; erosion can happen faster (3–6 months of unenforced violations).

## Related Loops

L-04 (US Deterrence Credibility Cycle), L-03 (Arab Normalization Reversal)

## Historical Range

- Post-Gulf War 1991: ~90 (peak credibility)
- Post-Iraq 2003 WMD failure: ~70
- Post-Syria CW 2013: ~55
- Post-Afghanistan withdrawal 2021: ~50
- Post-Iran nuclear threshold 2025 (no response): estimated ~40–45
- 2026 US-Iran War (credibility reset attempted): unknown trajectory
