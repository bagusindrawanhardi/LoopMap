# Relationship: Iranian Proxy Attack Rate -> US Military Response Intensity

- source: Iranian Proxy Attack Rate
- target: US Military Response Intensity
- polarity: Positive
- mechanism: When Iran-backed proxy attacks on US forces/allies increase, US military doctrine requires proportional or escalatory response to maintain deterrence credibility; sustained attack campaigns trigger strike operations, force buildup, and sanctions escalation
- delay: 1–4 weeks (tactical response); 3–6 months (strategic force repositioning)
- confidence: High
- evidence: E-05-us-centcom-posture-2025.md — CENTCOM 2025 Posture Statement; E-08-houthi-red-sea-attacks.md — Houthi attack-response cycle; E-11-iraq-pmf-fragmentation.md — PMF attack patterns
- counterarguments: US may absorb limited attacks without full response if escalation risk outweighs deterrence benefit (risk-tolerance asymmetry)
- related_loops: L-01, L-04

## Extended Description

The trigger-response dynamic is well-established: Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping (Nov 2023 onward) prompted Operation Prosperity Guardian and 170+ US/UK strikes on Yemen. PMF attacks on US bases in Iraq/Syria (160+ attacks Oct 2023–Feb 2024) triggered strikes on Kataib Hezbollah facilities. The pattern shows roughly 1–4 week lags for kinetic response, with strategic repositioning (additional carrier groups, pre-positioning) taking 3–6 months. The key nonlinearity is the threshold: attacks below a certain frequency/lethality threshold may be absorbed without response to avoid escalation, but once a threshold is crossed (US casualties, disruption to critical trade), response becomes politically mandatory.
