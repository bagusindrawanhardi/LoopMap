# Relationship: US Military Credibility Index -> Regional Ally Confidence in US Commitments

- source: US Military Credibility Index
- target: Regional Ally Confidence in US Commitments
- polarity: Positive
- mechanism: Regional allies (Israel, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Jordan, Iraq's non-PMF government) assess US credibility through demonstrated willingness to use force, consistency of red-line enforcement, and political stability of US commitments across administrations; when the US fails to enforce stated red lines (Syria chemical weapons 2013; Iran nuclear breakout without response 2025), ally confidence drops, triggering independent security-seeking behavior
- delay: 3–12 months (ally recalibration is deliberate but relatively rapid)
- confidence: High
- evidence: E-14-us-credibility-deterrence-rand.md — RAND deterrence credibility assessment; E-12-turkey-nato-regional-role.md — ally hedging behaviors
- counterarguments: US security guarantees are backed by the world's most capable military — allies may express doubt rhetorically while maintaining structural dependence
- related_loops: L-04, L-05

## Extended Description

RAND's 2024 analysis identified a credibility trap: the US had allowed multiple "red lines" to be crossed without full response (Syria CW use 2013, Iran nuclear advances 2021-2025, Houthi shipping attacks without decisive degradation), signaling to adversaries that US threats were negotiable. Allies (especially Israel and Saudi Arabia) responded by increasing independent military capacity and diplomatic hedging — which itself changes regional dynamics. Jordan's King Abdullah explicitly said the US "must demonstrate strategic commitment" to maintain alliance architecture.
