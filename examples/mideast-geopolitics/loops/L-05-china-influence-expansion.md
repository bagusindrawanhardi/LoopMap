# Loop L-05: China Regional Influence Expansion

- loop_type: Reinforcing
- variables: Hormuz Strait Closure Duration -> Gulf State Hedging Toward China -> China Regional Influence Index -> Gulf State Hedging Toward China
- narrative: Hormuz closure demonstrates US inability to guarantee Gulf security, accelerating Gulf state pivot toward China; as China accumulates influence through economic/diplomatic engagement, Gulf states deepen their China relationship further — a reinforcing expansion of Chinese regional power at US expense
- dominant_period: 2023–2026 (accelerated by US-Iran War Hormuz closure from Feb 2026)
- delay_points: 6–18 months for diplomatic reorientation; 12–24 months for structural influence building
- leverage_points: US restoring Hormuz access decisively; US offering new security architecture equivalent to NATO Article 5 for Gulf states; China overextending and provoking Gulf state concern
- collapse_conditions: Loop slows if: Iran-Saudi conflict directly threatens China's Gulf energy supplies (forcing China to take sides); US-China cold war pressure forces Gulf states to choose; Gulf states discover limits of Chinese security commitments (China has no mutual defense treaties in the region)

## Narrative

China's 2023 Saudi-Iran reconciliation brokerage was the template for this loop. Beijing achieved in weeks what years of US pressure could not — a formal diplomatic normalization between the region's two arch-rivals. While the reconciliation subsequently frayed (Iran's Feb 2026 Hormuz closure affected Saudi oil exports too), the precedent demonstrated China's growing role as a credible regional mediator.

The Hormuz closure (Feb 28, 2026) is the most powerful accelerant: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, and Kuwait all depend on Hormuz for oil exports. When the strait closed, Gulf states lost export revenue and faced the stark reality that US military presence had not prevented closure. This naturally drives them toward China — which is both their largest customer and a state with strong incentives to ensure oil flows.

**The key compounding dynamic**: as Gulf state-China relationships deepen (yuan oil payments, Chinese infrastructure investment, Chinese arms purchases, SCO membership), China accumulates structural leverage that makes further deepening attractive — network effects of geopolitical alignment.

**Limitation**: China has no mutual defense treaty in the region and has been historically reluctant to deploy military force outside its neighborhood. Gulf states are aware they are buying diplomatic influence, not security guarantees — which limits how far the loop can run.
