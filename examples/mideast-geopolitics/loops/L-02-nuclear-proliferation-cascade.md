# Loop L-02: Nuclear Proliferation Cascade

- loop_type: Reinforcing
- variables: Iran Nuclear Program Acceleration -> Saudi Nuclear Threshold Interest -> Regional Nuclear Proliferation Risk -> US Deterrence Erosion -> Iran Conventional Military Degradation -> Iran Nuclear Program Acceleration
- narrative: As Iran accelerates its nuclear program (driven by conventional military degradation), Saudi Arabia moves toward nuclear threshold, triggering regional cascade that erodes US deterrence architecture, which in turn reduces constraints on Iran's nuclear ambitions — a runaway proliferation dynamic
- dominant_period: 2026–2030 (emerging, not yet dominant)
- delay_points: 6–24 months for Iran nuclear advancement; 12–36 months for Saudi threshold investment; 24–60 months for regional cascade to materialize
- leverage_points: New nuclear agreement with Iran (restored JCPOA+ with verification); US extended deterrence upgrade (nuclear sharing or formal security guarantee to Saudi Arabia); preemptive strike on Iranian nuclear sites (high escalation risk)
- collapse_conditions: Loop contained if Iran accepts verifiable nuclear deal; or if massive US military victory destroys Iranian nuclear infrastructure comprehensively; or if regional actors conclude nuclear weapons bring more risk than security (very uncertain)

## Narrative

The Libya-DPRK lesson drives this loop. Iranian decision-makers have explicitly internalized that Gaddafi gave up WMDs and was killed; Kim retained them and survived. The US-Iran War 2026 dramatically strengthens Iran's nuclear rationale: conventional military degradation (IRGC facilities struck, air defenses degraded) makes nuclear deterrence the only reliable survival guarantee.

The cascade to Saudi Arabia is confirmed by MBS's explicit 2023 statement: "If Iran gets a nuclear weapon, we will get one too." Saudi Arabia has already built the latency infrastructure (Qassim research reactor, KACARE nuclear agency, civil nuclear agreements with multiple powers that deliberately exclude the "gold standard" enrichment ban).

The cascade doesn't stop at Saudi Arabia. Egypt has the largest Arab military and would not accept permanent nuclear inferiority to the Gulf states. Turkey, as a NATO member, faces the paradox of being in an alliance with nuclear weapons but lacking them — Erdogan has hinted at interest. UAE already has Barakah nuclear plant.

**The key nonlinearity**: once two or more states cross the nuclear threshold, deterrence in the region fundamentally changes character — from a US-managed extended deterrence to a fragmented multipolar nuclear order with no established crisis management protocols.
